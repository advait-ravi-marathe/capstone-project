# AI/FDE roadmap repair v2 — proposed architecture

Prepared 2026-10-28. Planning artifact only: no repository changes, board changes, public posts, applications, purchases, cloud resources or exam bookings are authorized by this file. All learner progress starts **Not started / Not assessed**. The user has 4+ years of software engineering experience and strong React/TypeScript plus Spring/Ktor experience; that is valuable prior experience, not proof of completed Python, cloud, AI or customer-delivery milestones.

## 1. The repair, in plain terms

**Latest user priority controls: passing interviews and switching jobs come first; the roadmap must continue into long-term depth afterward.** Existing FE/full-stack applications and role-specific preparation start now, using truthful existing experience. AI/FDE readiness is an early checkpoint in a longer career, not a reason to postpone the job switch, a finish line for learning, or a guarantee of absolute mastery/irreplaceability. The earlier delivery-first 210-day estimate is superseded by the capacity contract below.

The former 548-day sequence had a real alignment defect: it made classical ML and small educational models a long prerequisite to the work current AI/FDE jobs actually ask people to deliver. The new core is **shipping reliable AI-enabled software into a constrained user workflow**, with applied foundations learned when they explain an implementation choice. Personally implementing models remains useful, but becomes a parallel depth strand rather than a seven-month admission ticket to real LLMs.

The new order is:

1. **CoverStory:** real pretrained generative inference estimated around learning Day 9 and a genuine document RAG vertical slice around Day 26 under the interview-first budget; the fully evaluated local prototype is estimated around Day 41, then robust evaluation, security and an authorized cloud deployment. These are capacity estimates, not completion dates.
2. **Developer Workflow Assistant:** bounded tool execution, a genuine MCP server/client integration, human approval, trace/evaluation-driven reliability, and safe operation. Begin only after the learner can debug the simpler retrieval system.
3. **Customer delivery and interview evidence:** discovery, scoping, real feedback where available, iteration, deployment/incident/handoff ownership, unfamiliar-repo work and communication. These start in small forms in the first month and become substantial later; they are not a final presentation-only add-on.
4. **StreamForge / role specialization:** a recommender/ranker and data/MLOps strand after the first end-to-end AI product. A small ranking experiment belongs in the core; a third flagship is not required before applying.

**One well-evaluated application is more valuable than three tutorial-shaped demos.** Do not require all three flagships, all certifications or all 548 old days before applications.

## 2. Role target: a common core, not the union of every senior job

The audited roles divide into three routes:

- **Nearer target: full-stack / generalist FDE and AI product engineering.** Strong software delivery, Python/TS, data/API integration, customer problem solving, secure deployment and communication. Existing SWE experience transfers well, but new evidence must be built.
- **Production GenAI / data-platform FDE.** Add robust RAG/agents, evaluation, cloud operations, data systems and credible real-user delivery. Some roles explicitly require prior industry production GenAI; a personal project cannot silently satisfy that history requirement.
- **Longer-term specialist or Staff/Principal FDE.** Examples include NVIDIA post-training/multi-GPU depth, Databricks senior Spark/multi-cloud depth, and Staff-level cross-team architecture/mentorship. These are optional specialization/experience goals, not beginner prerequisites or titles earned by finishing a calendar.

Research sources: [frontier-company audit](frontier-fde-requirements-20261028.md), [enterprise-company audit](enterprise-fde-role-audit.md), [interview/visibility audit](interviews-and-visibility-research.md), [active vendor certification audit](vendor-certification-matrix.md). Each preserves exact official URLs, location/level restrictions and verification limits. Recheck roles at application time. Geography, work authorization, travel tolerance and exact target level are application filters, not reasons to postpone learning.

**180 minutes total per learning day; no extra lanes.** While switching jobs, default to **60 minutes DSA + 30 minutes role/certification bank + 90 minutes AI engineering**. Within that bank, reserve two 30-minute AWS certification sessions per seven learning days; the other 150 minutes are target-role interview preparation. Role preparation starts in week 1, not at the end of the AI roadmap: FE/React/TS/browser, backend/SQL/integration, HLD/LLD, unfamiliar-code/debugging and real-work behavioral/project stories. Its mix follows the actual target role and observed gaps. Existing FE/full-stack applications need not wait for AI or certificates.

### Recalculated scope: roughly 270 learning days, not the old 210 promise

The earlier design allocated 420 hours to AI/delivery, including 20 hours of late interview work. Move those 20 hours into the recurring role-preparation bank, remove the old 15-hour calendar-specific visibility assumption, and retain **325 hours substantive AI/delivery + 60 hours genuine buffer = 385 hours**, plus visibility on the actual schedule. AI holds 325 h substantive work, 60 h buffer, 19 h weekly visibility and about 1 h rounding reserve. This yields an **approximately 270-day core**; phase-window rounding can put the final gate around Day 271. It is an estimate, not a deadline or hiring prerequisite.

The 30 first-release AI dependency units still need 58 hours including 8 hours buffer, plus weekly visibility; we do not squeeze their original 90-minute primary tasks into smaller labels. **First 30 actual learning days contain 45 AI hours (43 unit-work + 2 visibility), 15 bank hours (11 interview + 4 AWS certification) and 30 DSA hours.** Real generation is estimated by Day 9, real persisted vector retrieval by Day 22, a real generative RAG vertical slice by Day 26. The full evaluated local prototype is estimated around Day 41. Only the **first 30-day starter scope** is the initial executable release; remaining P1 work is a later planned contract. Do not label 41 days of work a completed first 30-day release.

Buffer is actual capacity, not another mandatory feature list. Heavy DSA, interview-near mode, runtime problems and failed gates consume capacity; carry unfinished AI units forward. **Re-estimate after Days 7 and 30**, using actual minutes, carryover, runtime capability, independent ownership and interview feedback. Adjust remaining scope/windows rather than adding hours or pretending a 90-minute task is validated. At 7 learning days/week, 270 days are about 39 weeks; at 5, 54 weeks, before interruptions/remediation. Strong role fit may justify applications immediately; no AI calendar controls that decision.

**Day 1 is the actual restart**, not October 28 automatically. Old dates do not imply completion. Anchor only the next 1–2 weeks of board dates after restart; DSA review clocks remain tied to actual attempts.

### Continuing beyond job readiness

- **Core, approximately 270 learning days:** practical AI/FDE delivery alongside job-switch preparation; applications and interviews can happen throughout.
- **Next 60–90 learning days, selected by evidence:** complete/extend StreamForge, a data-platform branch, a relevant AWS credential or production/pilot gaps. One branch, not the union of all senior JDs.
- **Long-term, potentially through the former 18-month horizon and beyond:** deeper model implementations, post-training/LoRA, distributed data/inference, cloud/platform expertise, sustained real-user production ownership, OSS/product influence and advanced certification when experience supports it. Keep useful historical 548-day material as an optional mapped backlog, not 548 mandatory daily tickets.

After a successful switch, re-budget based on the actual new role, interview obligations and available capacity; do not assume extra hours. Durable depth requires continued learning, feedback and real responsibility. No finite roadmap makes a person literally irreplaceable.

### Foundations and long-term mastery depth

Mandatory applied foundations run alongside delivery: Python/types/errors/async; SQL joins/aggregation/windowing/query plans; API/data validation; vectors/similarity; train-validation-test/leakage; probability/metric uncertainty; linear/logistic baselines; overfitting/regularization; embeddings versus generation; transformer/context/token intuition; sampling/structured outputs; retrieval evaluation; throughput/latency/cost; and reliable service failures. A small personally implemented model/metric builds understanding; it does not precede every useful app.

Preserve selected older model-building lessons as a progressive depth strand. After the first deployment, expand toward ranking/ML experimentation, then need-driven fine-tuning/post-training, model serving, distributed systems or data-platform internals. AdvancedNVIDIA/Staff/Principal requirements are long-term specialization/experience benchmarks, not universal beginner prerequisites. A core FDE should explain when prompting/RAG/tools are insufficient and what data/evaluation would justify training; they need not train a foundation model from scratch first.

## 6. Readiness and application checkpoints

Existing suitable frontend/full-stack applications need not wait for AI learning at all. For AI/FDE applications, use an honest role-specific gap map; these checkpoints are operating guidance, not permission barriers or promises of an offer.

- **R0, restart:** record baseline, existing career evidence, target-role family and unknowns. No new learning milestone is complete.
- **R1, around Day 41 only if P1 evidence passes:** explain and modify the local generative RAG app independently; show actual evaluation and failure handling. Suitable for an early portfolio review, not a production/cloud/customer claim.
- **R2, during P2 only if relevant gates pass:** begin/expand applications to roles compatible with demonstrated level, geography and experience. One deployed, evaluated, secure full-stack AI application plus coding/design evidence is a meaningful checkpoint; do not wait for the second/third flagship. If cloud is blocked, target roles honestly and retain the missing capability.
- **R3, during P3 only if tools/MCP and safety gates pass:** broaden toward agentic AI/FDE roles; demonstrate real tool behavior, evaluation and operational judgment. This still does not supply required years of production GenAI or customer embedding.
- **R4, through the core only when evidence supports it:** repeatable performance across coding, unfamiliar repo/debugging, system design, AI evaluation/delivery, discovery/ambiguity and behavioral/project explanation; a real-user pilot where possible and truthful distinction from production.

For the six interview gates, use the research report's proposed 0–4 rubric. Pass means **two different unseen exercises on separate days**, at least 3/4 in critical dimensions, no critical correctness/security/integrity failure, and preferably at least one human/peer review. These are our rehearsal thresholds, not published employer cutoffs. Tool/AI allowance must match the practice objective and later actual interview rules. The learner must be able to operate without the assistant supplying the finished solution.

Maintain separate evidence levels: **local demonstration, real cloud deployment, real-user pilot, production operation, enterprise/organizational impact**. None automatically implies the next. Seniority and stated experience requirements are never “completed” by the roadmap.

## 9. Dependencies, no-cost paths and honest blockers

### Real inference without authorizing spend

Default to a **real local pretrained generative model**, selected from an official distribution with a suitable license after measuring the available machine. A quantized small instruction model may be practical on a CPU machine with sufficient RAM/disk; a smaller model can prove mechanics but may not meet the quality gate. A local runtime such as Ollama/llama.cpp is an implementation choice to verify, not a promise that every machine can run an adequate model. Record model revision, quantization, context limit, hardware and observed performance. Local embeddings + Qdrant's persisted local mode (or another genuine supported vector store) provide a concrete no-service-fee retrieval path; later server/container deployment is a separate skill.

If hardware, download/network permission or model capability is inadequate, record a **real-inference blocker** and request an explicit bounded hosted-API/compute option. Continue independent Python/API/data/evaluation work meanwhile. Do not replace real outputs with mocks and mark the gate passed. Local inference has resource/electricity costs and is not evidence of managed-provider API integration, enterprise inference or AWS deployment.

### Live cloud is not optional evidence for cloud-required roles

Designs, local containers, LocalStack and IaC validation are useful preparation but do not satisfy a live-cloud gate. A user-authorized AWS account with bounded workload/quotas and billing controls, or an authorized real vendor sandbox that permits the relevant operations, can supply the required evidence. Record sandbox limitations: a provisioned lab that hides IAM/networking does not prove independent IAM/network design. AWS budget alerts are not guaranteed hard spending caps. Use bounded application requests/tokens, quotas where supported, short-lived resources, alerts and teardown, and disclose residual billing risk before provisioning. No free-tier assumption authorizes charges. Require account/region/service permissions, an approved spend ceiling, bounded workload/quotas, alerts and teardown procedure (with residual billing risk acknowledged) before provisioning; retain blocked status until the real evidence exists.

### Real customers and production cannot be fabricated

A peer review is a peer review. A role-play is simulated discovery. A small real-user pilot is not enterprise production. Where access is unavailable, finish the portfolio/skills work and show the gap; seek a genuinely authorized work/internal/volunteer opportunity later rather than inventing adoption, revenue or operational history.
