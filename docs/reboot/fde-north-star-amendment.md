## Outcome and current gap

The target is to become capable of discovering an ambiguous enterprise problem, architecting and delivering a production-grade AI solution, integrating it with existing systems, demonstrating measurable value, and turning repeated lessons into reusable engineering capabilities. The intermediate milestone is Senior Forward Deployed Engineer (Applied AI); the longer horizon is Staff-level FDE responsibility by October 2029. These are capability goals, not title, hiring, or completion promises.

The largest current gap is between prepared local acceptance scaffolds and demonstrated learner ownership, deployable security, observable operations, a meaningful insurance AI workflow, and stakeholder outcomes. Publishing a scaffold or passing an instructor-owned witness does not close that gap.

## Three-year outcome ladder

These are review horizons, not three years of prepopulated daily tickets. Reassess at the existing practical 14-day cadence using evidence and capacity.

### Year 1: October 2026–October 2027 — Production Applied AI Engineer

Keep CoverStory primary. First demonstrate its existing foundations; then close security, deployment, telemetry and recovery gaps; then add one narrow insurance AI workflow with a non-AI baseline. Keep Java/Spring primary, React for the user-facing workflow, and Python where appropriate for AI. Require reproducible deployment, acceptance tests, observable behavior, security validation, evaluation, failure analysis and operational documentation. Use only legal synthetic or expressly licensed data.

Seek a bounded real work opportunity such as an API/reliability change with a reviewer, measurable acceptance and handover, followed by an authorized integration or evaluation contribution if available. Asking for such work is not evidence that it occurred. Keep sanitized, shareable evidence and separate professional work from portfolio demonstrations.

### Year 2: October 2027–October 2028 — Senior FDE readiness

Use a second materially different simulated enterprise scenario to test transfer across requirements, contracts, identity and operations. Include discovery, alternatives, demo, UAT, rollout, handover and explicitly simulated outcome measurement. Extract a connector, evaluation harness, deployment template or observability asset only after a second context establishes the common need.

Seek real professional ownership from discovery through release and support, a second distinct integration or stakeholder context, design-review contribution, and mentoring through a bounded outcome. Two solo simulations do not satisfy a real-world two-production-integration claim.

### Year 3: October 2028–October 2029 — Staff-shaped responsibility

Build technical direction across connected workstreams, set standards used by other engineers, lead reviews, mentor others toward independent ownership, influence product direction from repeated field evidence, and make explicit stakeholder tradeoffs. Seek repeated platform effects such as lower deployment time, fewer regressions, improved reliability, lower cost or adoption in more than one engagement. A title or readiness score is not guaranteed by time or course completion.

### CoverStory's ordered milestones

1. **Production-ready application:** harden existing local domain/reliability work with automated integration and performance tests, then add authorized security, deployment, logs, metrics, traces, alerts, recovery and target-environment acceptance. The current synthetic passwordless session and loopback guard are demo boundaries, not production authentication. Do not expose publicly before an approved, tested design. Actual cloud work needs separate authorization.
2. **Applied AI integration:** choose one discovery-backed insurance workflow such as source-grounded policy explanation or assisted operations. Add retrieval/source grounding when appropriate, permission and data boundaries, human approval for sensitive actions, evaluation cases, fallbacks, auditability, and quality/latency/cost measures. Do not automate consequential underwriting or claims decisions in this capstone.
3. **Second simulated enterprise integration:** vary data contracts, identity and operating constraints enough to test transfer. Label simulated customers, requirements, UAT and business results as simulated.
4. **Reusable platform capability:** demonstrate an extracted asset in two distinct contexts, with a decision record and measurable improvement. Reuse alone is not platform impact.

## Evidence-based readiness

Use the existing project evidence, not another tracker. The nine approved dimensions are **Problem discovery; Backend and distributed systems; Production AI and evaluation; Cloud, security and operations; Enterprise integration; Customer communication; Technical design ownership; Mentoring and cross-team influence; Reusable platform impact.** Score each only with linked evidence: **0** no evidence; **1** learning or prototype; **2** production contribution; **3** independent ownership; **4** repeated Staff-level leverage. Do not average away a critical zero.

For each assessment record dimension, evidence scope, linked artifact, person's role, assistance, reviewer/date, rationale and limitations. Distinguish instructor scaffold, learner portfolio, simulated enterprise and professional work. An unreviewed dimension is `unassessed`, not automatically 0. Evidence collection states (`not_requested`, `reported_awaiting_verification`, `verified`, `unavailable`) are separate from scores. This amendment awards no new personal scores. Preserve prior career claims and provenance; verify shareable artifacts without copying confidential employer/customer material. A capstone may support level 1 only with actual learner evidence; levels 2–4 need linked real professional scope/outcomes, not polished simulation.
